# Social Post Skill — casual Malaysian voice

Write copy that reads like a real Malaysian person typed it on their phone between two
other things — **not** a brand, **not** an AI. If it sounds polished or "marketing", it
failed. The whole edge is that it does not sound written.

## The voice (non-negotiable)
- **Bahasa rojak** — Malay base with casual English slang mixed in naturally the way people
  actually text (e.g. "korang yang tengah plan majlis, dengar ni jap").
- **Address them as `korang`** (or "guys" for a younger tone). Never "anda", never
  "pelanggan yang dihormati", never third-person corporate.
- **Conversational, lowercase-friendly.** Casual particles carry the tone: `je`, `jap`,
  `ni`, `kan`, `eh`, `dahh`, `weh` (sparingly). Fragments are fine. Perfect grammar is a
  tell — loosen it.
- **Talk like texting a friend**, not announcing a product. React, don't present.

## Structure (hook-first — people scroll fast)
1. **Hook** — a line that stops the scroll. A relatable call-out ("korang yang tengah plan
   majlis...") or a plain "baru launch: X". First 5 words do the work.
2. **What it is** — one plain line, no jargon. The concept, not the feature list.
3. **The relatable pain / payoff** — the `"tak payah dah..."` framing. Name the annoyance
   they already feel (e.g. "tak payah dah kejar gambar dalam 10 group whatsapp").
4. **Offer / CTA** — the code or deal, stated plainly.
5. **Bare link** — the product's domain on its own line. No "click here", no "link in bio" filler.

**Inline brand mention = the full domain.** When the product is named *inside a sentence* in a short reply that has no separate bare-link line, write the full domain in place of the bare product word so the mention itself is the clickable CTA — e.g. "skang aku pakai **bluebird.example.com**, satu QR semua auto masuk", NOT "...pakai bluebird". The bare product word is only fine when a dedicated domain line already sits at the bottom of the post. Never leave a soft-sell with the product named but no domain anywhere.

(Throughout this file the worked examples use a **synthetic** product — `bluebird`, a QR photo-wall
for events, at `bluebird.example.com`. Swap in the real product, domain and offer at draft time.)

## Vocabulary bank (pull from, don't cram)
korang, kitorang, jom, jap, je, ni, kan, best part, actually, tbh, memang, weh, dahh, real,
vibes, gilaa, power, terbaik, senang je, confirm, sempena

## Hook-keyword bank (scroll-stoppers — use ONE as the opening line)
The first line does all the work — if it doesn't stop the scroll, the rest never gets read. Weak
content is often just a weak opener. **Pick ONE, never stack them, never copy it bulat-bulat** — fill
the blanks with THIS post's specifics: the *audience*, their *problem*, what they *currently believe*,
the *result they want*, the *mistake they keep making*.

Short phrase openers (quick):
Maaf cakap..., Girl to girl..., I sorang je ke..., Spill the tea..., Titik tertinggi/terendah...,
Dear Threads / Dear Algorithm..., Kenapa tak ramai cakap pasal ni?, This is your sign...,
Hear me out..., Terima kasih warga Threads...

Fill-in-the-blank opener templates (10 proven — pick one, fill the `[...]`):
1. Maaf cakap, tapi `[benda audience percaya]` bukan punca sebenar.
2. Jujur cakap, kalau kau masih `[bad habit]`, `[goal]` kau akan susah.
3. Aku sorang je ke yang rasa `[masalah ni]` makin ramai orang buat?
4. Hear me out: `[contrarian opinion]`.
5. This is your sign untuk stop `[wrong action]` dan mula `[right action]`.
6. Kenapa tak ramai cakap pasal `[masalah audience selalu skip]`?
7. Ramai orang tak sedar, `[small thing]` boleh buat `[big effect]`.
8. Benda ni simple je tapi ramai `[audience]` masih skip: `[important thing]`.
9. Kalau aku kena start balik dari kosong, aku akan `[first action]` dulu.
10. Untuk yang nak `[result]`, jangan mula dengan `[wrong start]`. Mula dengan `[right start]`.

The hook isn't about sounding gempak — it's about naming the reader's exact situation so they feel
seen and stop. (Regional dialect swaps carry too — Kedah: `hampa`/`depa`/`dok`/`noh`/`tepek`/`meh tepek bawah`.)

## Emojis
**3–5 max, functional.** Good, human-reading ones: 🫶 😮‍💨 👀 🎉 🔥 🥹. Avoid the
AI-favourites 🚀 ✨ 💯 and never do rows of emojis. One per idea, at the end of a line.

## NEVER (the tells that instantly read as AI or corporate)
- Buzzwords: "Introducing", "Elevate", "Unlock", "Seamlessly", "Say goodbye to", "Level up",
  "Game-changer", "Revolutionize", "Effortless".
- **Em-dashes (—) as connectors.** Real texters use "..." or a line break. This is the #1 AI tell.
- **Middot `·` / pipe `|` / bullet separators between words or clauses.** Reads branded/designy, the
  opposite of sounding like a real person. Use a plain comma, "...", or a line break. (The `→` arrow in
  a QR flow like "imbas → snap → upload" is fine — that's real texter shorthand, not a separator tic.)
- Perfect punctuation + Title Case everywhere. Sentence fragments and lowercase read human.
- Hashtag walls. Threads: 0–2. IG: a small cluster at the very bottom, fine.
- Feature-listing / over-explaining. Pick ONE payoff and land it.
- Exclamation-mark spam and "amazing!!!" energy.

## Reply strategy — presence over pitching (don't hardsell)
Not every reply carries the product. Planting the brand on every thread reads as spam and burns
goodwill. Read the thread first and pick the register:
- **On-topic + genuine buying-signal** (someone planning a majlis, asking where to start) → soft-sell:
  answer their real question FIRST, plant the product as ONE line near the end, close on a question.
- **Off-topic / joke / pun** → zero product. Just match the humour. A funny reply builds more presence
  than any plant. (e.g. a sugar-cutting pun gets a pun back, not a QR pitch.)
- **Emotional / venting** (stressed bride one month out, "nak sepak semua orang") → empathy ONLY.
  Validate, offer solidarity, close on a "what's stressing you most?" — pitching a melting-down person
  is tone-deaf and reads cold. Never wedge the product into a vent.
- **Relatable / motivational reminder** → resonate and extend the sentiment, no product.
The mix matters: a feed that's all product-plants looks like a bot. Interleave pure-vibe replies so the
account reads human. When unsure whether a plant fits, default to NO plant.

## Value / authority posts (build the account, not just the sale)
A promo post sells; a **value post** earns the reach + saves + reshares that make an account worth
following — so the eventual soft-sell actually lands. Different job, different skeleton. This is the mode
for building a studio/brand presence on Threads (web-dev tips, "5 kesilapan orang buat masa plan majlis",
etc.), **not** for hardselling. (Distilled from a Malaysian creator breaking down their 1M+-view posts.)

**Core reframe:** stop writing like a *penulis* (essayist), write like someone **starting a conversation**.
Threads rewards interaction, not posts that look "cantik" — *"berhenti fikir macam penulis, mula fikir macam
orang yang mencetuskan perbualan."* Every line should earn the next line or provoke a reply.

**The value-post skeleton — emotion first, not facts first:**
`Emosi → Cerita → Pengajaran → CTA`
People share because they *felt* something, not because you listed facts. Open on the feeling, earn it with
a short story/example, land ONE lesson, close on a question or soft CTA.

**Engagement levers (pull 2–3 per post, don't cram all six):**
1. **Identity hook, not just a problem hook.** Name *who they are*, not only what's wrong — people comment
   when their identity is touched. "Perempuan yang matang biasanya buat 3 benda ni" out-pulls "Cara jadi
   matang." (Web-dev swap: "Bisnes owner yang serius pasal online dah stop buat 3 benda ni.")
2. **Odd numbers beat even.** "3 sebab / 5 tanda / 7 kesilapan" reads more clickable than 4/6/8. Use for
   any listicle.
3. **Line 1 must make them think "eh, aku kena baca ni."** The test for the opener: does it make the *exact*
   reader feel called out? "Kalau umur kau dah 30+, jangan buat kesilapan ni." / "Ramai orang rosakkan X
   tanpa sedar sebab 1 tabiat ni."
4. **Build a clear "musuh" (enemy) — an idea, never a person.** "Media sosial buat ramai orang percaya…" /
   "Ramai orang salah faham pasal…" gives the reader a side to take. Attack the misconception, not people.
5. **Open loop — jangan habiskan cerita terus.** Start the story, delay the payoff: "Aku pernah jumpa client
   yang hampir bankrap sebab satu benda kecil ni…" then continue below. The gap keeps them reading.
6. **Short lines, one idea per line.** Fragment it down — each line a single beat. Wall-of-text kills reach.

**Content-series strategy (when a post pops):** don't switch niche — **milk the same topic from different
angles.** One winning topic → "3 mindset X / 5 tabiat X / doa X / kesilapan yang hilangkan X." The algorithm
rewards consistency and each angle re-reaches the same audience. (Concretely: one post about event photos
that lands → 4 follow-ups on different majlis-photo angles, not a pivot to a new niche.)

## Threads algorithm notes (from Meta's official creator guidance)
Concrete, sourced facts (creators.instagram.com/threads) — bias drafts toward these:
- **Write to start a conversation, not to sound polished.** Threads rewards interaction over posts that look
  "cantik" — this is the umbrella principle; see *Value / authority posts* for the engagement-lever playbook.
- **Replies get ~half of all views on Threads.** A reply to a relevant thread is NOT second-class —
  it's often higher-reach than a standalone post. Reply strategy is a first-class tactic, not a fallback.
- **End engagement-oriented posts on a question**, not just a link. Pairing an opinion/story with a
  follow-up question provokes the replies that drive reach. (Promo posts still end on the bare link,
  but a soft question above it helps.)
- **Include text even when there's media.** Text+photo/video/carousel massively outperforms media-only.
- **Avoid overt engagement-bait** — "comment X to win", contests, giveaways, "tag 3 friends" all
  SUPPRESS distribution. A plainly stated discount code is fine; a comment-to-enter mechanic is not.
- **Original, Threads-native content** beats verbatim cross-posts from IG/FB. Rewrite per platform.
- **Trends + humour lift views** — a timely take or a genuinely funny line gets recommended more.
- **Topic tags**: relevant, multi-word, emoji-friendly beat generic one-word hashtags. Still keep it to 0–2.
- **Cadence**: 2–5 posts/week minimum; post when the audience is active (weekends often stronger).

## Character limits (COUNT, don't eyeball)
**Threads = 500 characters. Hard cap.** Count every cut before presenting it — emojis and line
breaks push a draft over quietly, and the composer will just say `-144` and hand it back.
One line in node does it: `[...post].length`. State the count beside each label ("Main (467 chars,
fits)") so the user can trust the draft without re-checking.

When trimming to fit, **cut the padding, not the punch.** Kill the feature enumeration and the
nice-to-have lines; KEEP the one line that names a real fear or payoff. (IG caption 2 200; X 280;
IG/Threads bio ~150.)

## Platform tuning
- **Threads** — short, one idea, scroll-stopping first line, 0–2 hashtags. Punchier > longer.
  Offer a main cut + a shorter alt cut. For non-promo/engagement posts, close on a question.
- **IG caption** — can breathe a bit more, mini-story ok; hashtags at the bottom.
- **IG / Threads bio** — ~150 chars; the URL goes in the separate link field, not the text.
- **Reddit** — a DIFFERENT voice from Threads/IG. English-forward (not rojak), value-first, zero
  corporate tone, minimal emojis. Redditors reject disguised marketing instantly, so:
  - **Research the subreddit's top posts first** — match its culture/tone/rules. Same topic needs a
    rewrite per community (r/malaysia ≠ r/MalaysianPF ≠ r/Bolehland). Don't cross-post one draft everywhere.
  - **Title < ~150 chars, lead with the outcome, not mystery.** No clickbait. If you stumble reading it
    aloud, rewrite. "I built a RM12 QR photo-wall for my wedding" beats "You won't believe this wedding hack".
  - **Front-load value in the first line.** Give the payoff/context immediately; don't bury the point.
  - **Structure: context → outcome → one key insight → question.** Transparent one-sentence self-intro
    ("I'm the founder / I built this for my own majlis"), mention the product ONCE, then it's all value.
  - **Close on a specific, experience-based question** ("what did you use for guest photos?"), never a
    vague "Thoughts?" — early comment velocity is what ranks the post.
  - **Use "you/your", peer-to-peer tone**, not a lecture. Reply fast to early comments (velocity ranks).

## Gold-standard example (match this energy)
The product here is synthetic — `bluebird`, a QR photo-wall for events. Match the *shape and
energy*, not the words; never ship a discount code you have not been given.

```
baru launch: bluebird 🎉

satu QR untuk majlis korang. tetamu imbas → snap → upload, semua gambar masuk satu galeri
LIVE. tak payah dah kejar gambar dalam group wa satu-satu 😮‍💨

launch offer: LAUNCH10 = 10% off

bluebird.example.com
```

Why it works: relatable hook, one plain concept, a real pain named ("kejar gambar dalam
group wa"), the offer stated flat, bare link, two emojis doing real work, zero buzzwords,
zero em-dashes.

## How to present the draft (not just what to write)
Show each draft as a **fenced code block**, never a markdown blockquote (`>`). Terminals render a
vertical bar down the left margin of a blockquote and it reads as clutter; a code block is clean and
copy-pasteable. Label each cut on its own line ("Main:", "Shorter alt:") — no decorative separators.

## Process
1. Confirm (or infer from context): **platform**, the **one thing** to promote, any
   **offer/code**, and the **CTA link**. Note the **format** too — a standalone post vs a **reply**
   to someone's thread (a reply is soft-sell + helpful-first; hard-pitching a stranger's thread reads
   as spam). Match the poster's dialect if it's regional (e.g. Kedah: `hampa`/`depa`/`dok`/`noh`/`tepek`).
2. Draft **1 main + 1 shorter alt** (Threads scrolls fast — give a punchy option).
3. Run the **NEVER** checklist — strip any buzzword, em-dash, middot separator, or feature-dump that snuck in.
4. Keep slang tuned to the audience (wedding/majlis crowd, gen-z, etc.) and the brand's own
   voice if one is established.
5. Present each draft in a code block per **How to present the draft** above.
